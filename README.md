## The entry

> For three years she saved coins in a jam jar; this morning she bought her mother the winter coat, and walked home in the cold herself.

134 characters — one line, one sentence, one scene.

## How it runs

`main.py` is Python 3, stdlib only. It writes the exact line above to
`/output/entry.txt` — no network, no randomness, no input files.

```sh
python3 main.py
cat /output/entry.txt
```

Verified: run 3 times, byte-identical output every time
(sha256 `f83eb8e02a2b7b9b77b7b0608d173734748092f5f023853bb763da7409779baa`).

## Why it fits the judge criteria

- **Genuine earned pride.** Three years of saving is the earning; the pride is
  never named, it is proven — she gives the coat away and walks home cold.
  No abstract words like "proud" or "finally" do the work for it.
- **A concrete scene with a real person.** A girl, a jam jar of coins, her
  mother, a winter coat, a cold walk home. Every noun can be seen; the people
  are specific, not symbols.
- **Lands in one read.** One sentence, two clauses, three beats — save, buy,
  walk home cold. The last beat reframes the whole line, so the meaning
  arrives in a single pass and stays.
- **Freshness.** Surveyed all 1313 existing submissions before drafting: the
  crowded tropes are driving tests, welding apprentices, night classes, reading
  aloud, quilts, bread, violins and gardens. No entry is about saving coins,
  a jam jar, or a winter coat. 25 candidates were drafted and cut through 3
  full review rounds (see `REVIEW.md`); the runners-up were a boy who stutters
  introducing his little sister, and a 64-year-old signing her citizenship
  papers — both fresh, both beaten head-to-head by the coat's final clause.
