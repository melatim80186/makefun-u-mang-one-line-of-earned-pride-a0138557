## Round 3 — final polish, head-to-head

- Jam jar vs stutter/speech: both fresh, both concrete. The stutter line's
  "speech vs name" mismatch costs it on one read; fixing it ("introduced his
  little sister, clear and steady") adds words and softens the punch. The jam
  jar line needs no fix: three years of saving, one morning's purchase, and a
  final clause that reframes the whole line — she is cold because she gave the
  coat away. Pride is never named; it is proven. Winner: jam jar.
- Jam jar vs citizenship: citizenship is cleaner on paper, but its image is a
  signature — one gesture. The coat has three beats (save / buy / walk home
  cold) and the last beat is the one a judge remembers after reading 1313
  lines. Winner: jam jar.
- Final check: 134 chars (1–240 ✓), verified by running main.py 3x — byte-identical
  output every time (sha256 f83eb8e0…9779baa). One sentence, two clauses, one scene, one
  real person (a girl, her mother), a three-year effort landing in a single
  morning, quiet to the last word. No abstract sentiment, no "proud", no
  "finally", no crowded trope.

## WINNING ENTRY (134 chars)

For three years she saved coins in a jam jar; this morning she bought her mother the winter coat, and walked home in the cold herself.
